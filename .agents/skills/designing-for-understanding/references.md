# References for `designing-for-understanding`

These notes justify the skill rules and give future editors a place to refine them without bloating `SKILL.md`.

## GOV.UK content design

- `What is content design?`
  - Good content design helps people find out what they need to know or do quickly.
  - Design starts from user needs, not what the publisher wants to say.
  - Depending on the need, you may need to reduce content, split one big piece into smaller pieces, or change format.
  - Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/what-is-content-design

- `Writing for GOV.UK`
  - Publish only what someone needs to know to complete their task.
  - The web reading path is: question → find page → understand → get answer → trust it → know what to do next.
  - Good online content uses short sentences, sub-headed sections, and simple vocabulary.
  - Technical terms are allowed, but explain them the first time.
  - Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/writing-for-gov-uk

- `Content types`
  - Make section titles active.
  - Do not use technical terms in section titles unless unavoidable and already explained.
  - Do not use `introduction` as the first section.
  - Do not use question headings or FAQ structures when clear structure would do.
  - Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/content-types/

## Microsoft Style Guide

- `Scannable content`
  - Readers face overwhelming volume; content should help them find what they need quickly.
  - Organize text into discrete components to support scanning.
  - Put first things first; above-the-fold content is the most likely to be read.
  - Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/scannable-content/

- `Headings`
  - Headings provide structure and visual reference points.
  - If readers do not read the headings, they likely will not read the text below them.
  - Headings should be specific, short, user-centered, and parallel at the same level.
  - Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/scannable-content/headings

- `Landmarks and headings`
  - Structural navigation must work for assistive technology users too.
  - Landmarks help users jump between major regions; heading levels help build a mental model of scope and depth.
  - Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/accessibility/landmarks-and-headings

## A List Apart

- `How We Read`
  - Readability is not just legibility; it includes the effort required to read.
  - Poor typography and layout can block understanding even when text is technically legible.
  - Reading behavior is shaped by context, needs, and scanning patterns.
  - Source: https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-read/

- `Testing Content`
  - Understanding is not the same as word recognition.
  - A person can decode every word yet still miss the intended meaning.
  - To test content, ask people to apply what they read, not merely say whether they understood it.
  - Source: https://alistapart.com/article/testing-content/
